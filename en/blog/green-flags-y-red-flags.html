Green flags and red flags: what the science actually knows.

The internet is full of “red flag” lists. Few of them are backed by evidence. Psychologist John Gottman’s research, built on decades of observing couples in the lab, is. Here’s what he found.

  • Stable couples keep at least 5 positive interactions for every negative one during conflict.
  • Couples still together six years later turned toward each other’s bids for connection 86% of the time; those who divorced, 33%.
  • The most powerful signal: feeling that the other person is committed to you (PNAS, 2020).

Chemistry shows up in the first five minutes. Compatibility takes longer to reveal itself. The good news: there are concrete, observable, well-studied signals that tell you whether a relationship has a future much sooner than you’d think.

Who John Gottman is and why he matters

John Gottman is an American psychologist who, since the 1970s, has studied thousands of couples in what became known as his “Love Lab”: he recorded their conversations, measured their reactions and followed them for years. According to the Gottman Institute, his team was able to predict which couples would split up with more than 90% accuracy. His findings now form the backbone of much of the couples therapy practiced around the world.

Red flags: the Four Horsemen

Gottman identified four communication patterns that, once they become habits, foreshadow the end of a relationship:

Of the four, contempt is the strongest predictor of divorce. If someone on a date talks with contempt about their ex, the waiters or “people,” take it seriously: that’s how they’ll talk when something displeases them.

The magic ratio: 5 to 1

Every couple argues. What sets stable couples apart is balance: during conflict they keep at least five positive or neutral interactions for every negative one, such as humor, an affectionate touch, validating the other person or acknowledging a good point. In everyday life, outside of conflict, happy couples’ ratio is even higher: about 20 to 1.

The most underrated green flag: how they respond to your bids

Gottman calls the small attempts to connect bids: “look at that beautiful sunset,” a meme, a “how was your day?” The other person can turn toward you, ignore you or turn against you. In his study of newlyweds, couples who were still together six years later turned toward those bids 86% of the time; those who divorced, just 33%.

You can watch for it from the very first dates: do they ask you questions back? Do they remember what you told them? Do they reply to your messages with interest or with one-word answers?

The number-one signal, according to 43 studies

The major analysis published in PNAS in 2020, drawing on 43 couples studies, found that the factor that best predicts relationship quality is how committed you perceive your partner to be. Next come appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner satisfaction and level of conflict. In plain terms: feeling that the other person is serious about you outweighs almost everything else. We explain it in what makes a relationship work.

Your first-date checklist

Green flags

  • They’re clear about what they want, and their intention matches yours.
  • They ask, listen and remember.
  • They talk about past relationships responsibly, without contempt.
  • They follow through: if they suggest meeting up, you meet up.
  • You feel calm, not anxious, after seeing them.

Red flags

  • Sarcasm or mockery aimed at you or at others.
  • They never take responsibility: everything was someone else’s fault.
  • They disappear and reappear without explanation.
  • They push to move fast or, on the contrary, avoid defining anything.
  • They ignore your bids for connection.

Frequently asked questions

What are Gottman’s Four Horsemen?

Criticism (attacking the person instead of the problem), contempt (sarcasm, mockery, acting superior), defensiveness (counterattacking or playing the victim) and stonewalling (shutting down and ignoring the other person mid-argument). Contempt is the strongest predictor of divorce.

What is the 5-to-1 ratio in a relationship?

It’s John Gottman’s finding that stable couples keep at least five positive or neutral interactions for every negative one during conflict.

What is the biggest green flag in a relationship?

Feeling that the other person is committed to you: according to the analysis of 43 studies published in PNAS (2020), perceived commitment is the strongest predictor of relationship quality. Turning toward your bids for connection also matters a lot.

How do you spot red flags on a first date?

Watch for contempt (how they talk about their ex, the waiters or “people”), whether they ever take responsibility, whether they ignore your attempts to connect, whether they disappear without explanation or push to move fast.

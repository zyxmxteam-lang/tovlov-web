Independent women and love: more options, more discernment.

They marry later, study longer and decide more freely than any generation before them. INEGI’s data tells the story of a deep shift in how Mexican women choose a partner.

By TovLov TeamOCT 16, 20267 min readMexico Data
In this article Women now study as much as men, or moreBoth work, both decideThey marry later, and choose betterThe “intimidating” mythKeys to love when you already have a full life
The essentials in 30 seconds
  • In 28.3% of couples where the woman is 15 to 49, she has more education than he does; in 25.9%, less.
  • In 52% of those couples, both partners work.
  • Women’s average age at marriage rose from 27.6 in 2014 to 31.7 in 2023.
  • The myth that they’re “intimidating” doesn’t hold up: what they want is a partner who matches them, not a provider.

“You’re too independent.” “Men are intimidated by you.” If you’re a woman with a career, projects and a mind of your own, you’ve probably heard it. The data tells a different story: you’re not the exception, you’re the trend.

Women now study as much as men, or more

INEGI’s ENADID 2023 compared the education levels of partnered women aged 15 to 49 with those of their partners:

It’s now more common for her to have more education than him than the other way around. The same holds for marriages in 2022: in 20.2% he had less education than she did, versus 17.8% where he had more.

Both work, both decide

In 52% of couples where the woman is of childbearing age, both partners work. Among marriages registered in 2022, the figure is the same: 52%. The “he provides, she cares” model no longer describes most young couples in Mexico.

They marry later, and choose better

Women’s average age at marriage went from 27.6 in 2014 to 31.7 in 2023. Behind it are more years of education, growing careers and, above all, a conscious decision: to marry when they want and whom they want, not when it’s “time.” We share all the data in at what age do Mexicans get married.

The “intimidating” myth

The problem is almost never that an independent woman is “intimidating.” It’s that she filters. With a full life, she no longer settles just to avoid being alone, and that narrows her options to the people who match her. That’s not a problem: it’s discernment.

You don’t need someone to complete you. You need someone to walk beside you.

The TovLov method

What does happen is that access gets harder: the clearer you are about what you want, the less random routes work for you. That’s why more and more professional women prefer to delegate the filtering to human, confidential processes.

Keys to love when you already have a full life

  1. Don’t shrink yourself to be liked. The right person celebrates who you are.
  2. Separate standards from superficial requirements: values, respect and commitment are non-negotiable; height and salary, not so much.
  3. Make room in your schedule for love, just as you do for your goals.
  4. Look at your patterns: are you drawn to unavailable people? Read about attachment styles.
  5. Look for men who are secure in themselves, who don’t compete with you but build with you.
  6. Use your best asset: clarity. Say it from the first date.

Frequently asked questions

Do women in Mexico have more education than their partners?

Increasingly, yes. Among partnered women aged 15 to 49, in 28.3% of cases she has more education than her partner, compared with 25.9% where she has less; in 45.8% they have the same level (INEGI, ENADID 2023).

At what age do women in Mexico get married?

In 2023, women’s average age at marriage was 31.7, up from 27.6 in 2014 (INEGI).

Is it true that successful women intimidate men?

The data doesn’t support it. What happens is that a woman with a full life filters: she no longer settles just to avoid being alone, which narrows her options to those who match her. That’s not a problem, it’s discernment.

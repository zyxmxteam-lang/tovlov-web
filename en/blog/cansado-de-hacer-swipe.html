Tired of swiping? It’s not you, it’s the model.

Apps have become the most common way to meet a partner, and at the same time the most exhausting. Here’s the data behind “swipe fatigue,” and what’s actually working.

By TovLov TeamOCT 16, 20267 min readDating

The essentials in 30 seconds
  • 39% of heterosexual couples in the US met online (2017), up from 22% in 2009 (Stanford).
  • 78% of dating app users have felt emotionally, mentally or physically exhausted (Forbes Health, 2024).
  • The number-one cause: failing to make a real connection (40%), followed by disappointment (35%) and rejection (27%).
  • What works: less volume, more filtering, moving quickly to meeting in person and leaning on a human filter.

You open the app “just for five minutes.” Half an hour later you’re still swiping, you’ve got three conversations going nowhere and a familiar feeling: this is exhausting. It’s not just you, and there’s an explanation.

The big paradox: the most popular route is also the most draining

A Stanford University study (Rosenfeld, Thomas and Hausen, published in PNAS) found that meeting online has become the most common way to meet a partner in the US: 39% of heterosexual couples who met in 2017 did so online, up from 22% in 2009. Among same-sex couples, the figure reached 65%.

At the same time, a Forbes Health and OnePoll survey of a thousand app users found that 78% have felt emotionally, mentally or physically exhausted by them, whether sometimes, often or always.

What exactly wears people out?

Look at the top cause: people don’t get tired of meeting people, they get tired of not connecting. The endless-catalog model has three side effects:

  • Choice overload. When it always looks like someone “better” is just a swipe away, it’s hard to invest in one person.
  • Shallow filters. A photo and three lines say nothing about values, emotional maturity or life vision, which is exactly what predicts whether a relationship will work.
  • Mixed intentions. The same app hosts people looking for something casual, for validation or for a serious relationship, and they don’t always say which.

7 ways to date without burning out

  1. Write down your intention. What are you looking for, and by when? If you don’t know, no one else will guess.
  2. Pick three non-negotiables and three flexible ones. Values, kids and lifestyle usually go on the first list; height, profession and taste in music on the second.
  3. Set a time limit. 20 minutes a day, max. Infinite swiping is product design, not destiny.
  4. Move to a call or coffee within a week. Endless chats build expectations, not connection.
  5. Prioritize in-person spaces with a filter. Events, trips and communities where everyone shares the same intention.
  6. Work on your patterns. If you always end up with the same type of person, the problem isn’t the app. Read about attachment styles.
  7. Delegate the filtering. A professional matchmaker does the most exhausting part: meeting, vetting and only introducing you to people worth your time.

Frequently asked questions

What is dating app burnout?

It’s the emotional, mental or physical exhaustion caused by dating apps: endless swiping, conversations that go nowhere, disappointment and rejection. According to Forbes Health (2024), 78% of users have felt it.

How many couples meet online?

According to the Stanford study published in PNAS in 2019, 39% of heterosexual couples who met in 2017 in the US met online, the most common route, as did 65% of same-sex couples.

What’s the alternative to dating apps?

Spaces that filter for intention: curated events and trips for singles, private communities with an entry screening, and professional matchmaking, where a human team gets to know and assesses each person before making an introduction.

Does matchmaking work better than apps?

For people looking for a serious relationship who are short on time, it tends to be more efficient: it eliminates swiping, verifies each person’s intention in an interview and filters for values and lifestyle instead of photos. No honest service can guarantee love, but it can promise fewer, better-chosen dates.
